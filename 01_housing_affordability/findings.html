Sydney vs Regional NSW:
The Affordability Gap is Flat

An analysis of rent, sales, and ABS Census data across 120+ NSW Local Government Areas (LGAs) reveals that buying markets are deteriorating in lockstep, while regional renters are being squeezed faster than those in Sydney.

Strategic Takeaway

The traditional "Sydney vs Regional" comparison hides the real story: housing costs are climbing everywhere at the same rate. Moving to regional NSW is not a magic fix—it just starts you at a lower price point. Beachside regional towns like Byron (17.1x) and Tweed (17.4x) now cost just as much as expensive city areas (like the Northern Beaches at 16.1x). Good-value houses are now only found far inland, where there are fewer jobs.

Data Constraints & Assumptions
Static Income Baselines

Small-area household income is derived from the ABS Census 2021 (last refreshed every 5 years). The same base incomes are used across 2024, 2025, and 2026. True household incomes have likely increased, meaning actual ratios may be slightly lower than computed.

Quarter Offsets

Rent metrics utilize March quarter data, while sales ratios use the preceding December quarter sales, aligned with DCJ's standard publication conventions. The minor lag is treated as negligible at annual granularity.

Amalgamation Proxying

Cootamundra-Gundagai Regional Council is matched with DCJ's "Gundagai" report entries to reconcile historical geographical reports with Census structures.

LGA Sample Exclusions

Low sales or rental volumes lead to data suppression by DCJ in some quarters. Ratios are compiled using the LGAs present in both comparison quarters to ensure trend consistency.

Sources: NSW DCJ Rent & Sales Reports (Issues 147, 151, latest available) · ABS Census 2021 (Table G02)
