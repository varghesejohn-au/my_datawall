National Overview & Sector Trends (2024 vs 2026)

Evaluating macro-level rating performance using matched-facility tracking to eliminate entry/exit distortions.

Sector-Wide Insight: While overall stars, compliance ratings, and staffing averages are climbing across Australia's ~1,700-1,800 matched facilities, Quality Measures (QM) have declined sharply (-0.48★). The percentage of facilities achieving 5-star clinical outcomes was cut in half, dropping from 22.6% to 11.6%.

Matched-Facility Star Rating Changes

Tracks identical facilities over time to identify true underlying quality shifts.

Key Trend Takeaways

  • Staffing Improvements: Staffing scores rose by +0.40★, driven by strict targets (facilities meeting RN minutes grew from 53% to 77%).
  • Compliance Expansion: The audit compliance sub-rating gained +0.39★, reflecting a sector where nearly everyone passes.
  • The Clinical Decline: Clinical Quality Measures (QM) dropped by -0.48★ despite improvements in Compliance and Staffing — a divergence that warrants investigation. The published data alone cannot determine whether this reflects outcome deterioration, methodology change, or both.
  • Sector Consolidation: The total national facility count declined from 2,620 to 2,591, consistent with broader sector consolidation; the dataset does not establish the specific cause of each exit.

This quality intelligence portal provides a rigorous look at residential aged care in Australia, compiled directly from Department of Health quarterly extracts (2023–2026).

By contrasting regulatory inputs with actual clinical outcomes and resident satisfaction, the project exposes the design limitations of the current star rating system, providing families and providers with actionable, evidence-based insights.

The Input vs. Lived Outcome Paradox

Investigating the correlation between standard regulatory inputs and the actual safety and comfort of residents.

Key Paradox Finding: Staffing and compliance show very weak relationships with broad clinical outcomes, while compliance is almost completely disconnected from reported resident dignity. A facility can pass an audit while residents report a weaker lived experience.

Resident Experience: The 2026 Weakest Links

National average percentage of residents answering "% Always" satisfied. Food quality is the clearest national resident-experience weak point.

State-by-State Rank Divergence

Evaluating state performance by comparing official badge ranks against actual clinical outcome ranks.

Key State Finding: NSW is the most under-rated state in Australia, ranking 5th on official stars but 2nd on actual clinical outcomes. Conversely, NT is the most over-rated state, ranking 1st officially but only 3rd on actual outcomes.

Official Rank vs. Clinical Outcome Rank Divergence

Positive numbers indicate the state is under-rated on the official star badge; negative numbers indicate it is over-rated.

Variance & Provider Types

ANOVA eta-squared analysis shows that provider type explains more than twice as much variance in the official star rating (η² = 0.059) as it does in actual clinical outcomes (η² = 0.026).

Government-run facilities score highest on the official overall badge (4.21★ avg) vs Not for Profit (3.85★) and Private for Profit (3.71★), but actual outcome differences are far smaller.

Methodology, Data Sources & Caveats

How the analysis was built, what is directly supported by the source data, and where conclusions should be treated cautiously.

01Data Sources
DATA SOURCES

Government Star Ratings extracts

Australian Government Department of Health, Disability and Ageing — Star Ratings quarterly data extracts. Four comparable quarters were used: May 2023, May 2024, May 2025 and May 2026.

Open official data collection
02Methodology
HOW IT WAS BUILT

Matched facilities and comparative tests

Facilities are tracked using Service Name + Provider Name because the published extracts do not provide a stable facility ID. The analysis tests trends, provider differences, staffing-vs-outcomes, compliance-vs-outcomes, weakest links, resident-experience dimensions and facility-level divergence.

03Coverage & Measures
ANALYSIS COVERAGE

What is actually measured?

The 2023 extract contains Star Ratings but no Detailed Data sheet. Staffing-minute and underlying clinical-measure analysis therefore covers 2024–2026. In 2026, 2,180 facilities had a complete published overall rating.

04Caveats & Limits
READ BEFORE INTERPRETING

Where caution is required

Correlation ≠ causation. The Staffing rating was redesigned in October 2025. Name/provider matching can break when services rename or change ownership. Analyst-defined risk flags are screening rules, not official government classifications.

Interpretation principleWhere the data cannot distinguish genuine outcome change from a rating-methodology change, this site reports the observed pattern and flags the uncertainty rather than presenting causation as fact.
